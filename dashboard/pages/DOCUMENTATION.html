1. Executive Summary & Problem Statement

The Defense Dilemma: Modern Integrated Air Defense Systems (IADS) deploy dense multi-static, frequency-agile surveillance, acquisition, and fire-control radar networks paired with supersonic surface-to-air missile (SAM) interceptors. In high-intensity Anti-Access / Area-Denial (A2/AD) contested environments, penetrating hypersonic strike and reconnaissance platforms face three existential challenges that legacy avionics cannot overcome:

  • 1. The Reaction Latency Chasm: Human electronic support measures (ESM) operators and legacy analog EW pods require multiple seconds to detect, digitize, classify, and formulate jamming responses. At hypersonic speeds (Mach 5.2, or 1.77 km/s), a 3-second lag allows enemy fire-control radars to close more than 5.3 kilometers of uncountered tracking.
  • 2. The Decoupled Control Penalty: In traditional military aviation, flight guidance (navigation) and electronic warfare (countermeasures) operate as completely isolated, decoupled subsystems. Physical high-G evasive maneuvers without electronic deception bleed vital kinetic energy and cause severe aerodynamic heating spikes. Conversely, electronic jamming without coordinated aerodynamic maneuvering enables monopulse Track-Via-Missile (TVM) and Home-On-Jam (HOJ) interceptors to score lethal proximity hits.
  • 3. Severe Aerodynamic & Thermal Bounds: Cruising in the upper stratosphere (29,500 m) at Mach 5.2 imposes severe structural dynamic pressure limits, structural load bounds (G <= 6.5g continuous, 10g emergency peak), and aerodynamic frictional heating constraints (T <= 1800 K).

What COG-EW Solves: COG-EW solves this challenge by introducing a Cognitive Multi-Domain Co-Design Architecture that dynamically couples RF signal intelligence, automated jamming resource allocation, and deep learning-guided flight trajectory planning into a sub-millisecond closed-loop OODA (Observe-Orient-Decide-Act) cycle.

6. Comprehensive Viva Defense Examination Questions & Answers

Q1: Is COG-EW a suicide drone or kamikaze weapon?
Answer: Absolutely not. COG-EW is an autonomous Hypersonic Reconnaissance and Standoff Strike Waverider cruising at Mach 5.2 in the upper stratosphere (29,500 m). Upon reaching terminal standoff range (within 220 km of the target bunker), it releases a dedicated hypersonic kinetic glide penetrator from its ventral weapons bay. The waverider never crashes; it initiates a supersonic climb (+10° AoA, ascending to 31,500m - 32,500m) and egress bank to safely exit contested airspace as a 100% reusable strategic asset.
Q2: What is the military and aerodynamic advantage of cruising at 29,500m at Mach 5.2?
Answer: At 29,500m (upper stratosphere), atmospheric density is less than 1.8% of sea-level density. This drastically reduces parasitic drag and aerodynamic frictional heating while maintaining sufficient dynamic pressure for scramjet combustion and waverider compression lift. Additionally, it places the aircraft above the engagement ceilings of short- and medium-range air defense systems, forcing adversaries to rely on expensive strategic SAM batteries.
Q3: Why does traditional decoupled EW and guidance fail during hypersonic penetration?
Answer: At Mach 5.2 (1.77 km/s), engagement windows last only tenths of a second. If electronic warfare operates without flight coordination, a high-G turn may expose the aircraft's maximum radar cross-section (RCS) specular flash directly toward enemy illuminators, or bleed vital kinetic energy into an unrecoverable stall. Conversely, jamming without flight maneuver allows monopulse tracking radars to guide interceptors to proximity detonation. COG-EW optimizes both domains concurrently.
Q4: What is the physical difference between Barrage Noise Jamming and RGPO Spoofing?
Answer: Barrage Noise Jamming is a brute-force Electronic Attack technique that floods the enemy radar's receiver bandwidth with high-power Gaussian noise (45 kW), reducing the signal-to-noise ratio to blind the receiver. Its drawback is high power consumption and beaconing the jammer's azimuth (Home-On-Jam risk). Range Gate Pull-Off (RGPO) is a deceptive countermeasure: it receives the enemy radar pulse, amplifies it, and retransmits it with progressively increasing time delays. The radar's automated range tracking gate locks onto the stronger false echo and 'pulls' away from the true skin return. Once pulled off, the jammer ceases transmission, leaving the radar with no target and breaking missile lock.
Q5: What are In-Phase (I) and Quadrature (Q) components, and why are they formatted as 2x1024?
Answer: Any bandpass RF signal can be expressed as s(t) = I(t)*cos(2*pi*f_c*t) - Q(t)*sin(2*pi*f_c*t). I(t) is the in-phase (real) component and Q(t) is the quadrature (imaginary, 90° phase-shifted) component. Downconverting to baseband and sampling both channels at 1024 discrete time steps captures complete instantaneous amplitude, phase, and frequency information as a 2x1024 real tensor, which directly feeds into the convolutional neural network.
Q6: Why is Peak-to-Average Power Ratio (PAPR) critical in radar and communications?
Answer: PAPR is defined as the peak signal power divided by average signal power: PAPR = max|s(t)|^2 / E[|s(t)|^2]. High PAPR signals (such as multi-carrier QAM or OFDM) require power amplifiers with wide linear dynamic ranges to avoid intermodulation distortion and spectral regrowth. Measuring PAPR allows our ESM engine to rapidly distinguish constant-envelope modulations (e.g., FSK, PSK) from complex digital multi-carrier modulations.
Q7: Why does the academic benchmark accuracy show 64.75% across the full dataset, but >91.9% in the operational regime?
Answer: The RadioML 2018.01A dataset includes samples from -20 dB to +30 dB SNR. At negative SNRs (-20 dB to -6 dB), noise power is 10 to 100 times greater than signal power, placing it beyond the Shannon channel capacity limit where even human radar operators cannot identify modulation. In actual military engagements, radar lock-on occurs at positive SNRs (>= +6 dB), where our Deep ResNet achieves 91.9% to 96.5% accuracy. Evaluating both envelopes demonstrates academic honesty and operational validity.
Q8: How is the Instituto IDF UAV flight telemetry dataset partitioned, and why?
Answer: The 120 recorded flights (>312,000 telemetry points) are strictly partitioned by entire flight laps to eliminate temporal and spatial data leakage: 60% Training Split (Flights 1 to 72) for model gradient backpropagation; 15% Validation Split (Flights 73 to 90) for learning rate scheduling and early stopping; 15% Testing Split (Flights 91 to 108) for untouched evaluation; and 10% Holdout Split (Flights 109 to 120) dedicated to 5-Fold Stratified Cross-Validation across independent 2-to-3 lap folds.
Q9: What is TCNResNet, and why is it superior to standard LSTMs for flight guidance?
Answer: TCNResNet is a Temporal Convolutional Network that uses 1D Dilated Causal Convolutions with residual connections. Causality guarantees that predictions at time t depend strictly on inputs <= t, eliminating future-to-past leakage. Exponential dilation factors (d=1, 2, 4, 8) provide a 20-step receptive field without recurrent looping. Crucially, TCNs process entire sequence windows in parallel on GPU tensor cores, achieving 0.027 ms latency—over 10 times faster than sequential LSTMs.
Q10: What caused the previous camera vibration bug, and how was it permanently fixed?
Answer: The simulation code previously checked 'if (mach > 4.5 || isEvading) cameraShake = Math.max(cameraShake, 0.18);'. Because the waverider's default cruise velocity is Mach 5.2, this condition evaluated to true on every single frame even when the simulation had never been started (status === 'ready'). This was adding random offsets to the camera position at 60 FPS. The fix forces cameraShake = 0.0 when idle or during smooth stratospheric cruise, reserving camera shake strictly for active kinetic detonations or high-G Split-S evasion maneuvers.
Q11: What do ADE and FDE metrics represent?
Answer: Average Displacement Error (ADE) is the mean Euclidean distance between predicted and ground-truth coordinates across all K forecast steps: ADE = (1/K) * sum(||p_k - p_hat_k||_2). Final Displacement Error (FDE) is the Euclidean distance at the final forecast step K (at T = +1.0s): FDE = ||p_K - p_hat_K||_2. Lower values indicate superior kinematic trajectory accuracy.
Q12: How does the backend communicate with the frontend?
Answer: The frontend communicates with the FastAPI backend via asynchronous HTTP REST endpoints: GET /api/simulation/state retrieves full vehicle, radar, and missile states; POST /api/simulation/tick advances physics by dt=0.1s; POST /api/simulation/reset re-initializes scenarios; GET /api/rf/classify-current executes real-time ResNet AMC forward inference; and GET /api/rf/sample/{index} extracts raw I/Q pulses from the RadioML HDF5 dataset.
